## 2. The full implementation roadmap

The following is the master backlog. All tasks begin as not started, except the planning artifacts explicitly established above. A task should move through these states:

- Not started: no verified work completed.
- Ready: dependencies and acceptance criteria are clear.
- In progress: an assigned owner is actively implementing it.
- Blocked: progress depends on a missing decision, resource, or upstream task.
- In review: deliverable exists and is awaiting verification.
- Done: acceptance criteria have passed and evidence is recorded.
- Deferred: intentionally moved to a later release, with a documented reason.

The roadmap uses work packages rather than assuming 154 different people are necessary. One qualified person can own several related tasks, while critical disciplines such as color science, security, accessibility, and quality assurance must still receive appropriate expertise.

# HOW TO USE THIS ROADMAP

Each task must have:

- **ID:** Unique task reference.
- **Status:** Not started, Ready, In progress, Blocked, In review, Done, or Deferred.
- **Owner:** One accountable person; other contributors may be assigned.
- **Priority:** P0 = launch blocker, P1 = important, P2 = enhancement, P3 = optional.
- **Dependency:** Work that must happen first.
- **Purpose:** Why the task exists.
- **Output:** What it must produce.
- **Connections:** Systems, teams, data, or tools it interacts with.
- **Acceptance criteria:** Observable conditions required to approve it.
- **Evidence:** Link to the approved document, design, code, test, or deployment.
- **Risks and decisions:** Outstanding questions or assumptions.
- **Release:** The version in which the task will ship.

A task is Done only when the deliverable is reviewed, acceptance criteria pass, and evidence is attached.

# CROSS-CUTTING REQUIREMENTS — APPLY TO EVERY PHASE

These are not isolated projects. They are rules that apply to every feature, integration, release, and operational process.

## A. Customer trust and accuracy

- Every product claim has a verified source and appropriate reviewer.
- Color previews clearly disclose screen and lighting limitations.
- Prices, availability, coverage, and specifications are not invented or silently assumed.
- Product recommendations do not imply compatibility without evidence.
- Safety and application guidance matches approved product documentation.
- Reviews and testimonials are genuine and appropriately represented.
- Error messages tell customers what happened and what they can do next.

## B. Accessibility

- Every interaction is keyboard-operable where applicable.
- Focus remains visible and predictable.
- Form fields have accessible names and clear errors.
- Content remains usable when zoomed and resized.
- Color alone does not communicate a state or instruction.
- Motion respects reduced-motion preferences.
- Accessible alternatives exist for maps, visualization, and other complex interactions.
- Automated scans are supplemented by manual assistive-technology testing.

## C. Performance

- Every image has an appropriate size, format, and responsive variant.
- Below-the-fold media is loaded appropriately.
- Critical assets are prioritized without overloading the initial page.
- JavaScript and third-party scripts are justified and measured.
- Layout shifts, long tasks, and slow API calls are investigated.
- Performance is measured on representative mobile devices and networks.
- Real-user monitoring supplements synthetic tests after launch.

## D. Security and privacy

- Least-privilege access is applied to every system.
- No secrets are committed to source control.
- Personal data is collected only when justified.
- Logs do not expose unnecessary sensitive data.
- File uploads are validated, secured, and subject to retention controls.
- Integrations use appropriate authentication and signed events.
- Data deletion, backup, and recovery behavior is documented.
- New vendors undergo security and privacy review.

## E. Maintainability

- Shared components are reused where doing so improves consistency.
- APIs and data contracts are documented.
- Critical logic has automated tests.
- Database migrations are reversible or have a recovery plan.
- Dependencies and licenses are tracked.
- Design and code changes are reviewed.
- Content and product data can be maintained without unnecessary developer involvement.
- Every critical process has an owner and a runbook.

## F. Commercial integrity

- Prices, discounts, stock, and shipping are sourced from authoritative systems.
- Tax and transaction requirements are reviewed for the launch market.
- Payment flows do not expose card data unnecessarily.
- Enquiries reach the right business team.
- Order and inventory records reconcile if commerce is enabled.
- Campaign claims and sustainability statements are evidence-backed.
- Marketing consent and opt-out behavior are respected.

# DEFINITION OF DONE — INDIVIDUAL TASK

A task is complete only when all applicable checks pass:

- Requirements and expected behavior are documented.
- Implementation matches the approved scope and design.
- Relevant unit, integration, and end-to-end tests pass.
- Accessibility and responsive behavior have been checked.
- Security and privacy implications have been reviewed.
- Error, loading, empty, and failure states work.
- Analytics events work where required.
- Documentation is updated.
- Code or content has been reviewed and approved.
- The change is deployed to the appropriate environment.
- Acceptance criteria have been verified.
- Evidence and accountable approval are attached to the tracker.

Not every task needs every check independently, but no applicable check may be omitted without a documented reason.

**P0 — Launch blocker:** Required for the approved launch scope, customer safety, business correctness, security, privacy, or core operation.

Priorities are provisional until customer research, architecture, costs, and business decisions are complete. Any item marked “P0 if in scope” becomes a launch blocker only if the corresponding business capability is approved for launch.

## 4. The most important quality standard for this particular website

Masterpiece must get five things exceptionally right:

1. Color trustworthiness: never present a digital swatch as a guaranteed physical match.
2. Product accuracy: every recommendation must connect to verified product specifications and compatible finishes.
3. Decision support: customers should understand which paint to choose, how much they need, and what to do next.
4. Conversion continuity: every major journey should lead naturally to a purchase, dealer, quote, sample, or useful resource.
5. Operational sustainability: the company must be able to update colors, products, content, and business information without rebuilding the site.

These five standards should influence the design, data architecture, engineering decisions, QA procedures, and business operations from the very beginning.
